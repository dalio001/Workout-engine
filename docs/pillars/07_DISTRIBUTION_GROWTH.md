### Research prompts to use later

Ask about the last real workout: what was planned, what changed, what information was missing, what happened to the record, and why the current tool was chosen. Observe the full workflow and ask what would make switching worth the effort. Test prototypes with imports, session interruptions, substitutions and next-session targets rather than only asking “Would you use this?”

Proposed target: routine-following self-directed strength trainees. Alternatives are notebooks/spreadsheets, simple loggers and adaptive programming products. Geography, OS mix, budget and team size are unknown; phase A resolves them. No interviews or outreach have yet been performed.

### Competitive inference, not a market verdict

[Strong](https://www.strong.app/) emphasizes simple tracking and access to training data. [Fitbod](https://fitbod.me/) describes personalized adaptive workouts. [Liftosaur](https://www.liftosaur.com/) supports programmable progression. Hevy combines logging, progress and social workflows and now includes adaptive guidance.

Test whether fast logging + migration + understandable adaptation + useful accountability provides a compelling combined experience. Do not claim these capabilities are absent from competitors. User preference and willingness to pay must be measured.

### Demand scoreboard

Use the exact definitions and thresholds in [../MASTER_PLAN.md](../MASTER_PLAN.md), section 9. The north-star measure counts meaningful repeat training; report one-session/week plans separately. Cohort retention and willingness to pay outrank total downloads, generated plans or likes. Targets are proposed hypotheses until a baseline exists.

### Rollout guardrails

Fix data loss/incorrect units before inviting more users. Repair retention before paid growth. Keep a useful solo experience before social cold-start expansion. Launch integrations one at a time. Publish only capabilities that passed their release criteria. Never treat the phrase “most demanded” as evidence of demand.
